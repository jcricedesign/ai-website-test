0→1 Product Design

I like the part before the answer is obvious.

I’m a product designer who works best in the early stages of a product—when the opportunity is interesting, the technology is promising, and what should actually be built is still unclear.

Turning ambiguity into something a team can build.

0→1 work rarely begins with a clean set of requirements. It starts with questions: Who is this really for? What problem are we solving? What does the technology make possible? What should we test first?

My work spans product strategy, interaction design, prototyping, and visual systems. I’m most useful when those things haven’t separated into different jobs yet.

The medium keeps changing. The underlying work doesn’t.

Microsoft · Xbox

Exploring what a connected gaming platform could become.

Across SmartGlass, Clubs, Game Help, Xbox on Windows, and related experiences, I worked on new interaction models before the patterns were established—turning platform capabilities into experiences people could understand and use.

Co-Founder · Product Design Lead

Finding the problem behind the problem.

What began as an idea about scheduling became a product centered on trust. Research changed our understanding of the opportunity—and changed what we decided to build.

Emerging experiences

Using prototypes to discover the product.

I build working experiments not simply to demonstrate an idea, but to learn what the product should be. Making something tangible exposes assumptions, constraints, and opportunities faster than discussion alone.

How I work

Understand

Talk to people, examine the environment, and question the initial premise.

Make

Turn abstract possibilities into prototypes, flows, and systems people can actually react to.

Learn

Use what we build to expose assumptions, constraints, and better opportunities.

Give it shape

Create enough definition and coherence that a team can move forward with confidence.

A pattern across very different products.

My career has moved through publishing, software, platforms, connected experiences, and new ventures. The common thread is figuring out how something works, finding the useful idea inside it, and making that idea visible enough for other people to understand and build.
