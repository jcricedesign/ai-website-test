What we learned trying to rethink the browser.

Orientation

This is not a proposal to rebuild Flowstate. It is a record of territory explored: what was built, what I saw in it, where ideas collided with reality, what survived, and which paths may be worth walking again.

Flowstate was an experimental Firefox-derived browser built around Views, ViewGroups, Workspaces, a River of current destinations, persistent Sessions, Mega Back, Set Aside / Restore, Focus states, and Companion. I joined after much of that foundation had been built. My principal contribution was as the replacement design lead: brainstorming with the team, helping synthesize and extend the work, connecting it to research and strategy, and presenting it to the larger organization.

People don't experience their work as tabs.

A browser exposes implementation objects: tabs, windows, history entries, bookmarks. People experience activities: researching, writing, planning, comparing, wandering, stopping, returning, and finishing.

Stopping isn't finishing.

Set Aside captured a particularly human state: I am not doing this now, but I am not done. Save the activity, clear the working surface, and make it possible to resume later.

Evidence / provenance
Recovered SessionManager source says: “Each window is its own session. Sessions may be saved, set aside and started at any time.” The build includes Set Aside behavior, session persistence, confirmation, telemetry, animation, and a dedicated sound.

Worth revisiting

  • Set Aside / Resume as general human-interface verbs.
  • Thing → View → Activity → Session → Thread.
  • Thread as the intention that survives multiple sessions.

Attention has bandwidth.

Every element that asks to be noticed spends a finite resource. The interface therefore has a volume, not just a layout.

Evidence / provenance
Contemporaneous notes include “USERS have a COGNITIVE CAPACITY LIMIT,” “noise/activity VOLUME,” “Calm state — calm search / Calm River,” and Focus In / Focus Out explorations.

Attention belongs to the person.

Software may suggest, remember, and assist, but the person decides what deserves attention. Focus should be self-defined rather than prescribed.

Worth revisiting

  • A Signal control that changes UI volume in coordinated steps.
  • Self-defined Focus states learned from repeated choices, then suggested rather than imposed.
  • Three understandable landmarks on continuous dimensions.

Attention has depth.

Visual art supplies a useful model: foreground is immediate and detailed; middle ground surrounds the subject and can approach; background remains in awareness but is quieter, slower, and predictable.

Software usually collapses this into visible/hidden or active/inactive. A richer interface lets information approach and recede without disappearing.

Spotlight / Daylight / Starlight

  • Spotlight — what has my attention now.
  • Daylight — surrounding context, available and capable of approaching.
  • Starlight — distant, stable orientation: goals and durable context.
Provenance
This was my interpretation of the system the Flowstate team had built, not the team's original organizing framework. It connected Focus + Companion, River + Mega Back, and an expressed goal.

Worth revisiting

  • Distance = where something sits in attention.
  • Prominence = how strongly it asks to be noticed.
  • Approach / Recede · Emphasize / Quiet · Expand / Compress · Reveal / Suppress.

Give activity shape.

Flowstate was not simply restyling tabs. StageManager maintained Workspaces, Views, ViewGroups, workspace history, current state, navigation direction, and a History Carousel.

Evidence / provenance
Recovered source defines View, ViewGroup, WorkspaceHistory, and StageManager. Back/Forward navigate the workspace View stack. History Carousel uses the internal name “megaback.” River implements overlapping ViewGroups, active state, spatial navigation, drag/drop, overflow, and animated separation.

Encounter with reality

Performance, discoverability, accessibility, browser architecture, and restoration determine whether a spatial model feels credible. The code even warms likely destinations before selection to preserve perceived immediacy.

Worth revisiting

  • Keep the behavioral grammar without assuming River's bubble treatment is final.
  • Navigate at multiple scales: current object, surrounding activity, previous session, durable thread.

Gathering meaning.

The earliest Firefox View exploration began with fragments: incomplete, disconnected, and distributed in value. The desired transformation was to remove noise, amplify signal, bring things into focus, connect them, and create value over time.

Information accumulates. Meaning is gathered.

Evidence / provenance
Original “Gathering Meaning” diagram; later Firefox View / “Book of You” pitch; Active Focus and visual-history explorations.

Encounter with reality

The data needed to infer relationships may be split across incompatible databases, retention rules, devices, services, and privacy boundaries. A concept diagram can imply relationships a database cannot represent, a sync system cannot carry, or a privacy model should not infer.

Worth revisiting

  • Signal = information relevant to the present intention.
  • Can accumulated context produce meaning without indiscriminate collection?
  • Can patterns be suggested while interpretation remains with the person?

Presentation is a user capability.

Customization usually means making software look the way I like. A stronger capability is: I can regulate how the digital world reaches me.

Type size, page width, content scale, density, saturation, contrast, motion, and prominence can help construct an environment appropriate to my state, energy, ability, environment, and intention.

Action → State → Behavior.

“Put notifications in the background” is an action. “Notifications are background” is a state. “New notifications arrive as tiny, quiet dots” is the resulting behavior.

Three landmarks

  • Attention — Starlight / Daylight / Spotlight.
  • Prominence — Quiet / Present / Assertive.
  • Focus — Open / Engaged / Deep.

Companion beside the activity.

Companion is broader than AI agent. A Companion is something or someone brought alongside an activity because they can contribute to it. The activity stays primary.

Evidence / provenance
The recovered build contains a dedicated Companion subsystem. My proposals included contextual specialist roles such as a Web Programmer and Designer after first encountering generative AI at Mozilla.
  • A real person on a call can be a Companion.
  • A specialist role: programmer, designer, researcher, editor.
  • A historical or philosophical perspective grounded in documented work.
  • A temperament: skeptic, optimist, critic, beginner.
  • Salon — several people, agents, roles, or perspectives temporarily assembled around an activity.

Encounter with reality

Context is powerful and dangerous. Privacy, consent, provenance, trust, model limitations, and access boundaries determine what a Companion should know or do.

The browser as lens.

Several sketches ask a larger question: what role does the browser play between a person and the digital world? The browser is not merely a renderer; it is an interpretation layer, a lens, and potentially a mediator.

Evidence / provenance
Handwritten explorations ask “What role does the browser play in this?”, describe the browser as a lens/window, and translate privacy/security into human questions such as “when are you safe?” and “what am I connected to right now?”

Worth revisiting

  • Design from human verbs rather than implementation nouns.
  • Make system state legible enough for people to exercise agency.
  • Treat privacy and safety as experienced states, not only settings.

What reality taught us.

The useful story is not “great ideas versus compromise.” Ideas encounter the world. Sometimes reality blocks them; sometimes it improves them; sometimes it reveals that the idea itself is wrong.

  • Technical reality — architecture, databases, synchronization, performance, platform constraints.
  • Privacy / trust reality — enough context to help can conflict with minimizing what the system knows.
  • Product reality — a beautiful interaction may be difficult to discover or explain.
  • Measurement reality — what can be measured may not be the hypothesis the team intended to test.
  • Organizational reality — staffing, ownership, strategy changes, deadlines, investment thresholds.
  • Temporal reality — sometimes the capability needed to make an idea viable simply does not exist yet.

Good question, bad answer. Right need, wrong metaphor. Technically impossible then. We were early.
