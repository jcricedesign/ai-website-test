Human
Interfaces

What happens when we stop teaching people the language of computers and start teaching computers the languages people already use?

Humans already have interfaces.

Hands. Eyes. Voice. Body. Space. Material. Sound. We have spent thousands of years learning how to point, touch, arrange, listen, gesture, make and understand one another. AI makes a different question possible: what if computation became literate in those languages?

Notes / Seeds

Color

Not every answer needs words. Could a small personal vocabulary of color communicate state, degree and uncertainty without demanding attention?

Sound

Pitch, rhythm, duration, harmony and silence already carry meaning through time. What if you and your computer developed a language that wasn't made of words?

Sign

The body is already capable of sophisticated symbolic language. The opportunity isn't to reduce sign language to UI gestures, but to make computation more capable of meeting people in languages they already use.

This is one conversation that became several ideas.

The images were generated during the discussion, not after it. Making the artifacts changed the questions: jewelry became Abacus; fabric became computational material; physical arrangement became Syntax; shared objects led to provenance and Oracle; composing people became Salon; layered paper became Substance; liquid metal became Quicksilver; Socratic questioning became adaptive intellectual resistance; sand suggested programmable topology; a shrug became evidence of intent. The work is preserved here at the point where each idea became useful enough to think with.
