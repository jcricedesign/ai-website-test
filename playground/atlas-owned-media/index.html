Product hypothesis · August 2026

Atlas +
Your Library

What if the TV stopped being a storefront and became a window into your own digital life?

Dump the mess here. We’ll help you make sense of it.

Years of photos, videos, music and documents tend to disappear onto drives and into folders. Atlas could help surface, review, organize, play and share that material across the screens already around you.

The wedge is not cloud storage. It is triage: turning an accumulated personal archive back into something useful.

The HDMI input is an open deployment surface.

A small computer can treat almost any television or monitor as a large, inexpensive canvas without inheriting the clutter, accounts and constraints of a smart-TV interface.

Large displayShared visual field for collections, playback and ambient information.
PhonePersonal second screen: browse privately, act almost eyes-free, or send objects up to the room.
TouchDirect manipulation: move media toward contextual actions such as save, share, delete or add to playlist.
VoiceSemantic control: ask Atlas to play, show, find, stop or act without navigating an interface.

One object model. Many inputs.

Review a pile of video on a touch surface → swipe objects into a playlist → use the phone as a remote → send the collection to the TV → “Atlas, play this collection.”

The interfaces are not separate products. They are different physical ways to operate on the same objects and actions.

Atlas is replaceable.
Your library isn’t.

Atlas is the framework: interaction, playback, search, device coordination and intelligence. The library is the user’s world: their media, collections and metadata.

The user’s content should remain portable and independently owned. Atlas should understand and operate on it—not own it. R2 is useful for the prototype; the eventual library could live wherever the user chooses.
