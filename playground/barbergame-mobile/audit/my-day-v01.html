What deserves the barber's attention now?

A working audit of My Day as BarberGame's temporal spine. This is not a UI specification. It is a place to test the product model before we systemize the interface.
Status: Testing
Central hypothesis
BarberGame organizes the barber's attention around Now. My Day operates the present. Business configures the future. Stats interprets the past. Other information enters the working day only when time, importance, consequence, and actionability make it relevant.

The working-day cycle

Select a moment. We are testing what should come forward, what should remain available, and what should disappear.

Candidate patterns

Patterns describe recurring product jobs. They are not yet components or visual treatments.

Attention is a budget

These are internal design states, not necessarily language shown to barbers.

Spotlight

Act now

Rare, consequential, interruptive when necessary. Arrival, cancellation, lateness, conflict, or another event with a meaningful action now.

Daylight

Operate now

The calm working field: current state, what is next, open time, breaks, and enough context to keep moving.

Starlight

Remember for later

Worth preserving but not worth interrupting. Business improvements, future tasks, trends, and ideas whose time has not come yet.

Promotion rule

Attention state is contextual.

Starlight → Daylight → Spotlight

Example: “Tomorrow's hours aren't configured” can be Starlight during a packed appointment day, Daylight after the final client, and potentially Spotlight when the missing hours are about to prevent bookings. Priority and attention are related, but not identical.

Interaction doctrine

The barber should never have to understand these rules. They should feel the result.

Questions to break the model

We should resolve these through scenarios and prototype behavior, not theory alone.
