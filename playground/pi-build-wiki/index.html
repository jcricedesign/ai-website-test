01

The idea

Keep the Pi running through a short outage in a small enclosure, with one internal M.2 NVMe SSD and the existing USB hard drive attached externally. The current turbine-style case would be replaced by the X1206-C1.

TOP · 01NVMe SSD + adapterAn M.2 NVMe drive mounts on an X1001 board above the Pi.
MIDDLE · 02Raspberry Pi 5Compute board, with a compatible cooler and the external USB drive.
BOTTOM · 03X1206 UPS + cellsFour 21700 cells in the UPS below the Pi, all inside the X1206-C1 case.

The external USB hard drive stays outside this case. If it takes its power from the Pi’s USB port, the backup must support its load too. If it has its own AC adapter, that adapter needs separate backup power to stay online during an outage.

03

Power path

Wall power → compact surge protector → suitable supply → X1206 power input → Pi 5 through the UPS pogo contacts. The NVMe board draws through the Pi; a USB powered hard drive draws through its USB port. Plug the supply into the UPS input, as Geekworm specifies, rather than the Pi’s USB-C input.

For X1206 V2.0, Geekworm specifies a USB-C supply rated at 5 V / at least 5 A, or 9–18 V DC / at least 3 A at its dedicated DC input. Follow the connector instructions for the exact board revision. A surge protector handles AC line transients; the UPS board handles the switch to batteries.

Clean shutdown takes setup

The X1206 can report power loss and battery state, but low-battery shutdown needs the software and power management configuration from Geekworm’s guide. Test that behavior before trusting the build with data.

04

Assembly checks

  1. Read the labels on the existing M.2 SSD: confirm NVMe interface, length, and capacity.
  2. Verify the X1206 board revision, correct input supply, matching unprotected cells, and cell polarity.
  3. Assemble Pi + cooler + X1001 + SSD + X1206 in the X1206-C1; check cable routing and lid clearance.
  4. Boot from existing storage first, confirm the NVMe drive appears, then decide whether to migrate the OS.
  5. Connect the hard drive and measure the complete load, including drive spin-up.
  6. Configure battery monitoring and low-battery shutdown; unplug wall power for a controlled outage test.
05

Runtime expectations

Four 5,000 mAh 21700 cells hold about 72 Wh nominal in total (4 × 5 Ah × roughly 3.6 V). That is stored cell energy, not energy delivered to the Pi. Conversion losses, battery cutoff, temperature, drive use, and cell age reduce usable runtime.

At a hypothetical 15–25 W system load, a rough planning range is about 2–4 hours. This is a calculation, not a measured X1206 result or a promise for this Pi and hard drive. The first real outage test should replace the estimate with a measured number.
